## 1. Acronyms and abbreviations
- Spell out the full form at first use in the document, or at first use in each chapter, paper, web page or article, followed by the acronym in parentheses. Use the acronym consistently after that.
- Introduce an acronym only if the term recurs. Don't overuse acronyms. If there are many, or they're technical, add a list of abbreviations.
- Use only the acronym the source institution uses. Never invent abbreviations.
- Write acronyms in capitals with no full stops: OECD, NATO, PISA (not W.H.O.).
- The English full form is in title case: Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA).
- Foreign-language bodies: give the English name, then the original name and acronym in parentheses: "the National Digital Strategy Office (Coordinación de la Estrategia Digital Nacional, CEDN)". If the body is mentioned often, the original acronym may be used.
- Plurals: add a lowercase s with no apostrophe: IOs, GVCs.
- Articles: use "the" with letter-by-letter acronyms used as nouns (the UN, the WHO, the EU). Don't use it with acronyms pronounced as words (UNESCO, NATO) or used as adjectives ("OECD experts").
- Abbreviations in figures and tables: only when space is tight, and explain them in the surrounding text.
- Image credits: © Author name/Image bank name, e.g. © J. Smith/Shutterstock.com.

## 2. Common abbreviations and signs
- Academic: cf., e.g., ed./eds., etc., et al., i.e., max., min., NB, ibid., n.d., No., p./pp., Q (quarter), Vol., vs., op. cit.
- Honorifics (no full stop): Dr, Drs, Prof, Mr, Ms, Mx (for someone who doesn't want a gendered honorific).
- Statistics: `..` missing value, `n.a.` not available, `x` not applicable, `0` nil or negligible, `|` break in series, `c` confidential, `e` estimated, `f` forecast, `p` provisional.
- Maths signs (`~ ÷ = > < + - ×`) take a hard space before and after.

## 3. Referring to the OECD
- Full name: Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, with British "Organisation" and a hyphen in "Co-operation". Give it in full at first use, then "OECD" or "the Organisation" (capital O).
- No personal pronouns for the OECD in formal writing. Write "OECD findings show that unemployment dropped in Q1", not "We find…". Write "Several OECD economists…", not "Several of our economists…".
- Membership vocabulary:
  - OECD Members, OECD Member countries, non-Members (capital M)
  - Key Partners: Brazil, China, India, Indonesia, South Africa (capitals)
  - accession candidate country: lowercase
  - For non-Members, prefer "partner", "country" or "economy". Use "economy" for sensitive territories.
  - Use "OECD area" for the geographic grouping, not "OECD Members": "including in the OECD area".
  - "observer" is lowercase.
- Official titles: Secretary-General, Deputy Secretary-General, Deputies Secretary-General; director, deputy director; chair, vice-chair, former chair; vice-chairperson; High-level Group. Don't alter their hyphenation.
- Use language and key facts from oecd.org/about when describing the Organisation.

  - the Council of Europe (CoE), separate from the EU

## 5. Countries and territories
- Use only names from the OECD Directorate for Legal Affairs (LEG) "List of names of countries and territories", in all content, published or internal. If in doubt, or asked to use a different name, the authority is LEGILU@oecd.org. You can't consult the list, so flag uncertain names for the user to check.
- Known OECD forms and common errors:
  - **Türkiye**, not Turkey. Long form at first mention: "The Republic of Türkiye (hereafter "Türkiye")".
  - **Czechia**, not the Czech Republic.
  - **the Slovak Republic**, not Slovakia.
  - **Korea**, not South Korea.
  - **China (People's Republic of)** in lists, sorted under "C". In text, "the People's Republic of China" at first mention, then "China".
  - **the Russian Federation**, sorted under "R".
  - **the United States**, **the United Kingdom**, **the Netherlands** (with "the", singular verb).
- First mention: the long name, the short name defined, then the short name.
- Nouns are spelled out (the United States); adjectives are abbreviated (US companies, UK unions, UAE delegates).
- Order: alphabetical in English, unless the LEG list says otherwise or there's a reason such as a ranking ("The top three performers were Japan, Canada and New Zealand").
- ISO country codes: generally avoid them in text. If needed, add a table of codes and names to the reader's guide.
- Visuals: use maps only from the LEG map model. Use flags only for countries, never territories, and check they're current. Never use emoji flags.

## 7. Territorial disclaimers and naming sensitivities
- **Mandatory**: any content with statistical data on Israel supplied by the Israeli authorities, any mention of Kosovo, and any reference to the Republic of Cyprus must carry the official territorial disclaimer or footnote from LEG. If you don't have the exact wording, insert a clear placeholder ("[Insert official LEG disclaimer for Israel]") and tell the user. Never paraphrase it.
- Sensitive names (e.g. Chinese Taipei, Cyprus, India, the Islamic Republic of Iran): follow the LEG list. If unsure, tell the user to check with LEG (LEGILU@oecd.org) and the Global Relations and Co-operation Directorate (GRCDIRPARTNERSHIPS@oecd.org).
