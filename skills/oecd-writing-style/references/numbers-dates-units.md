## 1. Cardinal numbers
- **One to ten in words**, 11 and above in figures.
- **Exceptions: use figures even for one to ten**
  - with ISO currency codes (`USD 6`)
  - with percentages (`5%`)
  - with units (`3 km`, `1 L`)
  - with million, billion and trillion (`3 billion`)
  - for scales (`on a scale of 0-10`)
  - when numbers below and above ten appear together: "12 Member countries and 3 non-Member economies"
- **Two numbers for one item**: spell one out. "four 1-metre stands".
- **Start of a sentence**: always words ("Twelve OECD Member countries…"). If that's clumsy, rephrase.
- **Titles and subtitles**: words ("Ten Years of PISA").
- **Web content**: digits throughout.
- **Roman numerals**: only in names, titles of works, events or periods (King Richard III). Write "20th century", not "XXth century".

## 2. Decimals and fractions
- Use a decimal point, never a comma. Always include a leading zero: `0.45`.
- Decimal quantities take the plural: "2.4 children", "0.7 children".
- Money: round to a whole number or give two decimals (`USD 0.02`, `EUR 0.20`).
- Non-decimal fractions in words, hyphenated: one-third, two-thirds, three-quarters. Use only familiar ones; otherwise use a percentage. Write "Over one-quarter", not "Over ¼". Write "25% of the territory", not "two-eighths".

## 3. Negative and ordinal numbers
- Negative numbers: minus sign with no space after it (`-446.2`). Use a non-breaking hyphen or a true minus. Currency: `CAD -225`.
- Use ordinals sparingly. Don't use "firstly" or "secondly"; restructure the sentence.
- Centuries use ordinals ("the 18th century", no superscript, lowercase "century"). Ages use cardinals ("55 years old"; "a 55-year-old manager").

## 4. Percentages
- `%` with no space, in text and graphics.
- Exceptions:
  - In publication titles, chapter titles and section headings, write "per cent".
  - At the start of a sentence, write "per cent" with the number in words: "Ninety per cent of participants agreed, but 10% did not."
  - Use "percentage" for a general share: "a small percentage of students".
- Never write "percent". Never use `%` with a number in words ("fifty %") or with a fraction ("¼%"); write `0.25%`.
- Percentage points: "percentage points (p.p.)" at first use, then "p.p.".
- Rounding: one decimal place. If any value in a sentence or list has a decimal, give whole numbers with `.0`: "2.2% in November, up from 2.0% in October."
- Repeat `%` after each number in a series: "45%, 55% and 65%".
- Ranges: "10% to 20%" (repeat with prepositions) but "10-20%" (one sign with a hyphen).

## 5. Large numbers and separators
- Four or more digits: separate thousands with a hard space, never a comma or full stop: `4 000`, `1 275 103`.
- No space in years, page numbers, legislation numbers or addresses (`2025`, `p. 1001`).
- Round in running text: `1.2 billion`, not `1 198 650 000`.
- Use the short scale: billion = 10^9, trillion = 10^12. French *billion* is 10^12, which matters when translating.
- Abbreviations for tables and figures: thsd (k), mln (M), bln (G), tln (T).

## 6. Currency
- Use the **ISO code** for a specific currency in publications, short content and web copy. Use the **term** for generic references: "the budget is USD 5 million" but "millions of dollars".
- Placement:
  - code before the amount with a hard space: `USD 1 000`
  - symbol before the amount with no space: `$1 000` (only for tight space in social media, figures or tables)
  - term after the amount: `1 000 dollars`
- Define uncommon codes at first use: "KGS 5 million (Kyrgyz Republic soms)". Give an equivalent in EUR or USD: "IQD 1 billion (USD 750 million)".
- Never mix codes, symbols and terms. Wrong: "AU$ 100 million … 100 million US Dollars … 100 million EUR". Right: "AUD 100 million … USD 100 million … EUR 100 million".

## 7. Units of measure
- Hard space between number and unit (`580 kg`), except `%` and `°C`, which are written as `20°C`.
- Never invent units. Never put them in bold or italics. Capitalise units named after people (A, Pa, W in kWh).
- Write "per" in text rather than a slash: "100 kilometres per hour". `km/h` is acceptable in tables. A slash is fine in ratios: `USD 100/t`, price/earnings.
- Standard symbols:
  - Length: mm, cm, m, km, in, ft, yd, mi
  - Area: m2, km2 (prefer km2 to hectares), ft2, sq yd
  - Weight: µg, g, kg, oz, lb, t (tonne)
  - Mt: million tonnes; spell out at first use, and use the symbol only if frequent
  - Volume: L (capital), hL, daL, pt, qt, gal
  - Temperature: °C (Fahrenheit is not used)
  - Time: s, min, hr, y
  - Energy and other: A, kA, Bq, J, kJ, kWh, V, W (lowercase "volt" and "watt" when spelt out), mph, km/h, px
  - Transport: pkm (passenger-kilometre), tkm (tonne-kilometre), vkm (vehicle-kilometre)
  - Emissions: t eq. CO2 (tonne of CO2 equivalent)

## 8. Dates, times and periods
- General text: `15 April 1998`, with a hard space between day and month. No ordinals ("15th") and no commas. Technical documents and datasets use `DD-MM-YYYY`.
- Write "15 November 2024 at 14:30 (UTC+1)", not "November 15th, 2024, at 2:30 PM". Use the 24-hour clock with no AM or PM, and give a time zone if relevant.
- Spell out days and months in text, titles and subtitles. Abbreviate only in tight tables and figures, or on social media:
  - Months: Jan., Feb., Mar., Apr., May, June, July, Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec.
  - Days: Mon., Tues., Weds., Thurs., Fri., Sat., Sun.
- Seasons are ambiguous across hemispheres, so use months or quarters. "In May 2018", not "in spring 2018". Use a season only when tied to a place or the weather ("The Seine floods more in winter").
- Decades: `1990s` (no apostrophe).
- Ranges: all four digits with a hyphen, `1998-2001`, also in publication titles (*OECD-FAO Agricultural Outlook 2024-2033*). Make the number of years clear from context.
- A period overlapping two calendar years (fiscal, marketing or academic year) uses a slash: `2025/26`. Two or more full years use a hyphen: `2017-2019`, not `2017/19`.
- Quarters: "the fourth quarter of 2024" in text; `Q2 2020-Q1 2021` for ranges; `Q1 2024` in tables and figures.
- Capitalise named eras (the Renaissance, World War II); lowercase general ones (the past decade).

## 9. Hard spaces (non-breaking, U+00A0; Ctrl+Shift+Space in Word)
Required:
- between a title and a name: `Mr Smith`, `Dr Jones`
- between a numeral and its unit or noun: `page 10`, `37 firms`, `19th century`
- within numbers of four or more digits: `20 000 students`
- between day and month (not month and year): `15 January 2003`
- in country names: `United States`
- in currencies: `EUR 50`, `USD 10 million`
- in units (not `%` or `°C`): `580 kg`
- with initials and abbreviations: `E.B. White`, `Smith, J. (2025)`, `e.g. [text]`, `et al.`
- before and after ellipses: ` … `
- around maths signs: `x = 2`, `> 5`

Non-breaking hyphens (Ctrl+8): use them in terms, proper nouns, numbers, negative sums, years, abbreviations and acronyms that must not split across lines. Don't replace every hyphen with one.
