## 1. British English spelling
- Use the Concise Oxford English Dictionary, with the language set to English (United Kingdom).
- -ise, not -ize: organise, realise, analyse, criticise.
- -our: labour, honour, behaviour.
- -re: centre, metre, litre.
- Doubled consonants before endings: travelling, focussed, labelled, modelling.
- Also from the guide: programme, licence (noun) / license (verb), practice (noun) / practise (verb), defence, offence, tonne, cheque, annex.
- Keep US spelling in names that use it: International Labour Organization, World Trade Organization.
- Watch English/French near-spellings: abbreviation, address, apartment, character, competitiveness, connection, contract, correspondence, defence, environment, example, existent, government, independence, medicine, messenger, negligible, negotiation, offence, parallel, recommendation, reflection, responsible, tariff, traffic, weekend.

## 2. Plain language
- Write for a global audience, many of them non-native speakers and non-specialists. Use short sentences, common words, and active verbs where possible.
- Avoid foreign words and expressions where English alternatives exist (see section 5).
- Choose the word that says exactly what you mean (see sections 3 and 4).
- Useful references: Plain English Campaign; plainlanguage.gov; Orwell's six rules; *The Economist* style guide.

| imply | suggest | infer | deduce |
| inter- | between | intra- | within |

| practise (v.) | do, rehearse | practice (n.) | custom, procedure |

| stationary | still | stationery | paper goods |

- assure (people), ensure (make certain), insure (against risk).
- autarchy (absolute sovereignty), autarky (economic self-sufficiency), autocracy (rule by one person).
- continual (frequent), continuous (uninterrupted), continued (lasting).
- Avoid "quite", "rather" and "somewhat" (imprecise), and "respectively", "the former" and "the latter" (they force re-reading).

## 4. Commonly misused words
| Word | Guidance |
|---|---|
| across | Often ambiguous ("data across countries"). Be specific: "compared the unemployment data of several countries". |
| address (v.) | Vague. Use "discuss", "tackle", "investigate". |
| all right | Two words. Never "alright". |
| and/or | Don't use. "A or B, or both". |
| annex | Always "annex", never "appendix". |
| comprise | "The report comprises six chapters." Never "is comprised of". It doesn't mean "include". |
| concerted | Needs more than one actor. |
| consensus | A shared opinion, not a majority view. |
| constitute | The parts make up the whole. Not "contain". |
| impact | A noun only. Not "the policy impacted pollution": write "had an impact on" or "affected". |
| implement | Overused. Try "carry out", "apply", "put in place". |
| in regard to | Not "in regards to". |
| include | Implies the list is partial. Never "including but not limited to". |
| literally | Only for something that actually happened as described. |
| loan | A noun. The verb is "lend". |
| presently | Avoid (it means "soon" in the UK and "now" in the US). |
| regardless | Never "irregardless". |
| regime | Avoid for governments (pejorative). |
| regular | At regular intervals, not "frequent". |
| scheme | Avoid (negative in US English). Use "programme", "plan", "initiative". |
| slated | Avoid (it means "criticised" in the UK and "scheduled" in the US). |
| state | Clarify: a country, a level of government, or the government (federal countries). |
| table (v.) | Avoid (it means "present" in the UK and "postpone" in the US). |

## 6. Plurals
- Mass nouns take a singular verb: advice, information, knowledge ("The information was shared").
- **"Data" is always plural**: "the data show".

## 7. Inclusive language
- Be neutral and factual, use the terms people use for themselves, and mention identity only when relevant. If in doubt, ask the stakeholders concerned (or COM).
- **Age**: "older people", not "the elderly" or "seniors". Balance comparisons: younger, middle-aged and older adults.
- **Disability**:
  - People-first by default: "people who use wheelchairs", not "handicapped people".
  - Use identity-first ("a Deaf person") when the community prefers it.
  - No "suffers from" or "victim of": "children living with Down syndrome".
- **Gender**:
  - Use gender-neutral terms unless gender matters. Write "different genders", not "opposite genders".
  - Avoid pronouns where possible: "finish the test", not "finish his test". Otherwise use singular "they".
  - Respect people's own pronouns. Use Mx where appropriate.

- **Race and ethnicity**:
  - Be specific: "policymakers from Japan, Korea and Thailand", not "Asian policymakers".
  - Use "minority" as an adjective: "minority groups", not "minorities".
  - Avoid outdated terms and ones that imply a value judgement.
- **Sexual orientation**:
  - "sexual orientation", not "sexual preference"; "LGBTIQ+".
  - Don't assume gender identity: "Marriages lead to new households", not "the marriage of a man and a woman".

## 8. English/French false friends and calques
Watch for these, especially when editing text drafted by French speakers.

| to change | changer | *change* = monnaie |
| secret | confidence | *confidence* = confiance |
| to ask | demander | *to demand* = exiger |

- Translation length, for layouts: French is +30% longer, Spanish +25%, Italian +20%, German and Russian +15%, Japanese and Korean -10%. Keep this in mind for web and social copy that will be translated.
