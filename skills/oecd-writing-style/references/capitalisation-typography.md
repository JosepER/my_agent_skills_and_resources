# Capitalisation and typography

## 1. General rules
- Capitalise the first word of a sentence and proper nouns: names of events, people, places, organisations, nationalities and languages.
- Specific terms may be capitalised. Generic references never are.
- Avoid excessive capitals. They slow reading.

## 2. Titles

| Type | Case | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Report or book titles and subtitles, periodicals, series, names of projects or initiatives | Title case | *Empowered Citizens, Informed Consumers and Skilled Workers: Designing Education and Skills Policies for a Sustainable Future* |
| Country notes (when using the parent publication's name) | Title case | OECD Services Trade Restrictiveness Index: Czechia |
| Report chapters, working papers, policy papers, policy briefs, case studies, short content | Sentence case | "Pooling our strengths: The power of stakeholder engagement in education and skills policy" |
| Boxes, figures, infographics, tables, headings, and references to report components | Sentence case | Table 1.4. Size and sectoral distribution of Thailand's central state-owned enterprise sector |

- **Title case** capitalises every word except co-ordinating conjunctions (and, but, or), prepositions (of, in, for, on, with…) and articles (a, an, the), unless that word comes first or follows a colon.
- **Sentence case** capitalises only the first word and proper nouns.
- In both, **capitalise the first word after a colon**: "Attracting talent: The (no longer) remote option for regions and cities".
- Title case with hyphenated adjectives: capitalise both parts (*Evidence-Based*). Never capitalise after a hyphen inside one word (*Co-operation*).
- Name specific components with a capital ("Chapter 3", "Table 1.4") but lowercase them generically ("a chapter discusses…", "the report includes a table…").
- Don't put dashes in titles. Spell out numbers one to ten, days and months in titles.
- "e-words": "e-commerce", but "E-commerce" at the start of a title or sentence.
- Keep "The" only when it's formally part of a name: *The Economist*, but the *New York Times*.

## 3. OECD-specific terms
- Capitalise: OECD Member(s), OECD Member country/countries, non-Member, Key Partners (Brazil, China, India, Indonesia, South Africa), the Organisation (meaning the OECD), Secretary-General, Deputy Secretary-General, Deputies Secretary-General.
- Capitalise Associate, Participant and Invitee when they describe a non-Member's status in an OECD body, in line with C(2012)100/REV2/FINAL.
- Lowercase: accession, accession candidate country, observer, partner, economy, and "country" within "Member country" (unless in a title).
- Lowercase roles used generically: director, deputy director, chair, vice-chair, former chair, vice-chairperson. Exception: "High-level Group".
- Lowercase "council" or "committee" used generically. Capitalise them when they're specific (the OECD Council, the Steel Committee). Note: "Council will review two resolutions" (Council as a body, no article).

## 4. Proper nouns, roles and institutions
- Capitalise official titles when attached to a name or referring to a specific office: "France's Minister of Finance announced…", "The Secretary-General addressed the OECD Working Party on Pesticides."
- Lowercase generic roles and bodies: "Several ministers and deputy ministers participated in the conference." "The government proposed a referendum."
- Capitalise named groups and institutions: the Working Party on Countering Illicit Trade, the Directorate for Financial and Enterprise Affairs.

## 5. Specific vs generic

| Specific (capitalise) | Generic (lowercase) |
|---|---|
| the Renaissance, the French Revolution, World War II | the past decade, for a century |
| the Internet, the OECD Intranet | a web page, a website, a web manager |
| The 1965 Road Act; the Privatisation Bill; the Open Government Directive | the act was passed; Congress is debating a bill; a directive |
| The OECD Council Decision on…; the Ministerial Declaration on…; the Resolution of the Council on… | their decision; a declaration; two resolutions |
| OECD Guidelines on the Protection of Privacy | new guidelines |
| the Ministerial Council Meeting | meets at ministerial level |

## 6. Compass points and seasons
- Capitalise compass points for named regions or in proper nouns and titles: the East Coast, the Northwest Passage. Lowercase them for directions: head south.
- No hyphen: Northeast, Southwest.
- Seasons are always lowercase (unless in a title): "the winter temperatures".

## 7. Italic, roman, bold and underline
- Use the O.N.E Author styles for headings and hierarchy, never manual bold, italics or underlining.
- **Italics**:
  - titles of reports, books, journals, newspapers, magazines, databases, and working and policy paper series, with no quotation marks around them (*OECD Employment Outlook 2025*, *The Economist*)
  - foreign words not naturalised in English (*autarky* in an economics context, *Secretaría de la Función Pública*)
  - technical terms or variables at first mention ("the variable *X*")
  - Latin abbreviations, except those listed under roman below
- **Not italic**:
  - common foreign terms (per capita, laissez-faire, ad hoc)
  - URLs
  - titles of chapters, sections, articles and papers (roman in double quotes: Chapter 4 is titled "Sustainable growth strategies in Europe")
  - names of programmes, legislation, codes, declarations and organisations, and proper names (the Paris Agreement, Deutsche Bundesbank)
  - For emphasis, use italics rarely; overuse looks informal.
- **Latin terms in roman**: ad hoc, cf., circa, e.g., et al., ibid., id./idem, i.e., loc. cit., nota bene (NB), op., op. cit., per annum, per capita, p.s., versus (vs.), via, vice versa.
- **Mixed text**: in an italic passage, items that would normally be italic switch to roman. Punctuation after an italic title stays roman unless it belongs to the title: *How's Life in the Digital Age?* keeps its question mark in italics.
- **Bold**: very sparingly, and only when meaning depends on it. It reads as aggressive when overused.
- **Underline**: never, except for automatic URL formatting.
