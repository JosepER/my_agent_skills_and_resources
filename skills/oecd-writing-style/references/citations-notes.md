# Citations, references, sources and notes

## Contents
1. In-text citations (author-date)
2. Reference list: author names
3. Reference list: formats by type
4. Sources for boxes, figures, infographics and tables
5. Notes (endnotes; notes in figures and tables)

## 1. In-text citations
- Use the author-date system, with optional page numbers. Every in-text citation must have a full entry in the reference list, and the list must contain nothing that isn't cited.
- One author: (OECD, 2016). Two or three authors: list all, (Deng and Mi, 2023). Four or more: first author plus "et al.", (Hardy et al., 2024).
- Several works: separate with semi-colons: (OECD/EUIPO, 2018; OECD/EUIPO, 2020; OECD/EUIPO, 2021).
- Name in the sentence: "Deng and Mi (2023) note that…".
- No date: (OECD, n.d.). Forthcoming: (OECD, forthcoming). Unpublished: (Smith, unpublished).
- OECD production (O.N.E Author/Mendeley) adds a bracketed subscript reference number: (OECD, 2016[1]). Include it only if the user works in that system or their text already uses it.
- Don't pile many citations into one sentence; integrate them. Don't make generic claims without a source. **Never put sources in footnotes or endnotes.**

## 2. Reference list: author names
- One author: Smith, J.
- Two: Smith, J. and M. N'diaye
- Three: Smith, J., M. N'diaye and A. Singh
- Four or more: Smith, J. et al.
- Editors: Rossi, P. (ed.); Ito, F. and H. Aalto (eds.)
- Institutions: the acronym, or the full name if there's none: OECD. Two or three institutions are joined by a slash: OECD/World Bank/IADB. Four or more: OECD et al.
- Don't put hyperlinks behind titles. Write the URL out at the end, preferably a DOI.
- Posthumous works may add † after the author's name.

## 3. Reference list: formats by type
Italic titles are shown as *Title*; quoted titles are in double quotation marks and roman type.

**Reports**
- Standalone monograph, periodical or technical report: `Author(s) (Year), *Report Title: Report Subtitle*, Publisher, City, URL.`
  - OECD (2025), *Does Healthcare Deliver?: Results from the Patient-Reported Indicator Surveys (PaRIS)*, OECD Publishing, Paris, https://doi.org/10.1787/c8af05a5-en.
  - OECD/FAO (2024), *OECD-FAO Agricultural Outlook 2024-2033*, OECD Publishing, Paris/FAO, Rome, https://doi.org/10.1787/4c5d2cfb-en.
- Report in a series: `Author(s) (Year), *Report Title: Subtitle*, Series Title, No. X, Publisher, City, URL.`
  - OECD/SWAC (2025), *Roads and Conflicts in North and West Africa*, West African Studies, OECD Publishing, Paris, https://doi.org/10.1787/77474489-en.

**Papers and articles**
- Policy or working paper: `Author(s) (Year), "Paper title: Paper subtitle", *Paper Series Title*, No. X, Publisher, City, URL.`
  - Slawomirski, L. et al. (2025), "The economics of diagnostic safety", *OECD Health Working Papers*, No. 176, OECD Publishing, Paris, https://doi.org/10.1787/fc61057a-en.
  - Without a series: OECD (2024), "The benefits of learning the difference between facts and faith: An impact assessment", OECD Publishing, Paris, URL.
- Journal article: `Author(s) (Year), "Article title", *Journal Title*, Vol. Volume/Issue, pp. x-y, URL.`
  - Bulliard, J. et al. (2021), "Breast cancer screening and overdiagnosis", *International Journal of Cancer*, Vol. 149/4, pp. 846-853, https://doi.org/10.1002/ijc.33602.

**Short content**
- Policy brief: `Author(s) (Year), "Title: Subtitle", [*Series Title*, No. X,] Publisher, City, URL.`
  - OECD (2024), "How do governments direct support for innovation?: Lessons from recent OECD measurement and impact analysis (MABIS) work", *OECD Policy Briefs*, No. 4, OECD Publishing, Paris, https://doi.org/10.1787/c1d93d1c-en.
- Country note or case study: `Author(s) (Year), "Title: Subtitle", Publisher, City, URL (accessed on Day Month Year).`

**Components**
- Chapter or annex: `Author(s) (Year), "Chapter title", in *Report Title: Subtitle*, Publisher, City, URL.`
- Box, figure, infographic or table: `Author(s) (Year), "Figure 1.5. Title", in *Report Title*, Publisher, City, URL.`

**Data** (for OECD Data Explorer, use the short link from "Share")
- Indicator: `Author (Year or n.d.), Indicator Title (indicator), URL (accessed on Day Month Year).` Example: OECD (2025), *Hours worked* (indicator), https://data-explorer.oecd.org/s/2db (accessed on …).
- Dataset: `Author (Year or n.d.), Dataset Title: Subtitle (dataset), URL (accessed on …).`
- Dataset group: `Author (Year or n.d.), Datasets Group Title (dataset group), URL (accessed on …).`

**Media**
- Newspaper: Author(s) (Year), "Article title", *Newspaper Title*, URL (accessed on …).
- Web page: Author(s) (Year), "Title of page", URL (accessed on …). Example: OECD (2025), "Environment", https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/environment.html (accessed on 7 May 2025).
- Social media: Author or handle (Year), Text of post, Platform, Day Month published, URL (accessed on …).
- Blog: Author(s) (Year), "Title", *Blog Title*, URL (accessed on …).
- Podcast: Author (Year), "Podcast title: Subtitle", *Series Title*, Duration: hh:mm, Publisher, City, URL.
- Video: Username (Year), "Title" [video], Day Month posted, URL (accessed on …).

**Special cases**
- Forthcoming: (forthcoming) in place of the year, with no URL.
- Unpublished: Author(s) (unpublished), "Title: Subtitle", with no URL.
- No date: Author(s) (n.d.), "Title: Subtitle".

**Legal**
- Include the issuing body, title and date, plus the URL and access date if available. Follow the domestic citation standard of the jurisdiction (and EU practice for EU acts).
- OECD legal instruments:
  - In text: OECD Recommendation on Responsible Innovation in Neurotechnology [OECD/LEGAL/0457].
  - Reference list: OECD (n.d.), *Recommendation of the Council on Responsible Innovation in Neurotechnology*, OECD/LEGAL/0457, https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0457 (accessed on …).
  - Make clear whether you mean the original instrument or a revision, and use the matching year.

## 4. Sources for boxes, figures, infographics and tables
- Every one needs a source below it, and the source also goes in the reference list. Use the short form: `Source: Author(s) (Year), Title, URL.`
  - Source: CERED/HCP (2024), Population Census, https://www.hcp.ma/….
- Adapted material: `Source: Based on OECD (2022), *Global Plastics Outlook*, Figure 2.2, URL.` Reproducing an external figure unchanged needs reproduction rights; a citation alone isn't enough.
- Never write "Source: OECD" or "Source: Authors". For OECD data published for the first time, cite the data itself ("OECD Survey of …", "Country consultation …").
- Sources are never given in notes.

## 5. Notes
**Endnotes in text**
- Use endnotes, not footnotes, numbered with superscript 1, 2, 3. In reports, number them consecutively by chapter; in papers and short content, number them throughout. In O.N.E Author, use the automatic notes function.
- Put the marker after punctuation, at the end of the sentence, or mid-sentence if the note covers only part of it: "…cross-border trade dynamics² and the availability of investment."
- Notes add commentary. They never give sources.
- Never put a note on a title or heading. Never put a figure or table in a note, or a note inside another note.

**Notes in boxes, figures, infographics and tables**
- Number them separately from the main text, inserted manually as superscript numerals.
- Never put a note on the title, give sources in notes, or nest notes inside sources or other notes.
- Order below the item: "Note:" or "Notes:" first, then "Source:" or "Sources:".
