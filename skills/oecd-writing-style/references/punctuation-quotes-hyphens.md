## 1. General
- Be consistent and use punctuation sparingly.
- In English, never put a space before `: ; , . ? !`.

## 2. Commas
- **No Oxford (serial) comma**: "soup, bread and salad". Add one only if leaving it out would confuse: "soup, bread and cheese, and salad".
- Set off non-essential clauses and appositives: "Mr Smith, the committee chair, recommended…". Prefer commas to parentheses for these.
- Put a comma before a conjunction that introduces an independent clause: "…its chemical structure, but quite another…".
- Put commas between coordinate adjectives (where "and" would also work): "swift, extensive changes".
- No comma:
  - after "i.e." or "e.g."
  - before parentheses
  - before or after en-dashes
  - before the final "and" or "or" (except as above)

## 3. Colons and semi-colons
- **Colon**:
  - Use it after an independent clause, to introduce a list, an explanation or an indented quotation, and to separate a title from its subtitle.
  - One per sentence.
  - No space before it.
  - Lowercase after it, except in titles.
  - Write "The team has one goal: success", not "one goal; success".
- **Semi-colon**:
  - Use it to join related independent clauses: "The committee reviewed the report; approval was unanimous."
  - Use it in lists whose items contain commas: "Paris, France; Rome, Italy; and Athens, Greece".
  - Not in simple lists. Don't swap it with a colon.

## 4. Dashes, hyphens and slashes
| Mark | Use |
|---|---|
| Hyphen `-` | compounds, ranges (`1998-2001`, `10-20%`), joint titles (OECD-FAO), city pairs (Paris-New York) |
| En-dash `–` | Spaced, in pairs, to set off a highlighted aside: "The conference – for which more than 1 000 participants had registered – lasted three days." A single one adds an explanation: "The meeting ran late – the discussion had been absorbing." An aside opened by a dash must close with one. |
| Em-dash `—` | **Never used in OECD work.** |

- No dashes of any kind in titles.
- Forward slash:
  - Use it for periods overlapping two years (`2017/18`) and for ratios (`USD 100/t`, risk/reward, CO2/km2).
  - Don't use it for "or". Write "ID card, passport or both", not "ID card/passport".
  - Don't use "and/or"; use "or", or "A or B, or both".
  - Don't use it for spans of two or more full years. Write `2017-2019`.
  - Don't use it to replace "per" with written-out units. Write "100 kilometres per hour".
  - Don't use it where a hyphen belongs ("Paris-New York flight").

## 5. Full stops, question and exclamation marks
- End every sentence and paragraph with a full stop, followed by a single space.
- No full stop after headings, titles or bullet items, unless the bullet is a complete sentence.
- Abbreviations: "etc.", "i.e.", "e.g." and "No." take a full stop. Contractions keeping the first and last letter take none: Mr, Ms, Dr, Ltd.
- Question marks only for direct questions. "She was asked whether she had read the report." takes a full stop.
- No exclamation marks in formal writing. Very sparingly in informal writing.

## 6. Apostrophes
- Singular nouns, plurals not ending in "s", and proper names (including those ending in "s") take 's: the minister's, the children's, James's.
- Plurals ending in "s" take the apostrophe after the s: the ministers' decision.
- Joint possession: the apostrophe goes on the last name only: "Jean and Emilia's office".
- No contractions (cannot, do not, they are), except on space-limited social media.
- No apostrophe in decades or plural acronyms: 1960s, IOs.
- Watch its/it's and your/you're.

## 7. Parentheses and square brackets
- Use parentheses sparingly; commas often work better.
- Full stop inside the closing parenthesis if the parenthetical is a full sentence: "(The OECD Council met last week.)". Otherwise outside: "…several topics (the latest report, new data and a project proposal)."
- Square brackets:
  - for asides inside parentheses
  - for words added to a quotation: "At this point [2022], the outlook is still unclear."
  - around an ellipsis marking an omitted paragraph: `[…]`
  - In OECD production files, the in-text citation number is bracketed and subscripted: (OECD, 2025[1]).

## 8. Quotation marks and quotations
- Double quotation marks for direct speech, short quotations, titles of chapters, papers and articles, and terms ("participants"). Single marks only for a quote within a quote.
- Punctuation placement:
  - inside the marks only if it belongs to the quoted material: The report concluded that "continued growth is uncertain. The outlook remains cloudy."
  - outside otherwise: The speaker said monetary policy must remain "vigilant".
  - The report discusses "grey markets", which are…
- Reported speech: "Taxes play a vital role in limiting the social and economic costs of smoking," OECD Secretary-General Mathias Cormann said.
- **Short quotes** (five lines or fewer): run into the text in double quotation marks, with an in-text citation: "… growth has remained stable," Mathias Cormann said (OECD, 2024).
- **Long quotes** (more than five lines): set as a separate indented paragraph with no quotation marks. Put the citation in parentheses at the end, with no full stop after it.
- Start the quote with a capital at the start of a sentence or after a colon. Use lowercase when it's part of a running phrase: The *Economic Outlook* argued that "some emerging market economies … are now in debt distress".
- Omitted words: ` … `. Omitted paragraph: ` […] `. Added words: `[ ]`.
- Always cite the source of a quotation. Don't change wording inside quotations. Flag apparent errors instead, and use [sic] only if necessary.

## 9. Ellipses
- Three dots with no spaces between them: `…`. Put a hard space before and after.
- Use them only to show omissions from quotations. Not for pauses, and not to replace "etc." or "and so on". Never in parentheses, and never spanning headings.

## 10. Ampersand
- Only in official names and established abbreviations: Johnson & Johnson, R&D. Never in running text: write "health and education".

## 11. Lists
- Use lists sparingly. Keep items grammatically parallel.
- Complete-sentence items: initial capital and full stop.
- Fragments: lowercase start, no end punctuation.
- Mark items with bullets, Arabic numerals, lowercase roman numerals (i, ii, iii) or lowercase letters, used consistently.
- Run-in lists inside a sentence take semi-colons: "PISA results show that: girls perform better in reading; boys perform better in mathematics; and…"

## 12. Hyphenation
**Fixed spellings**

| Write | Not |
|---|---|
| policymaker, policymaking | policy-maker, policy maker |
| decision maker, decision making | decision-maker |
| macroeconomic(s), microeconomic(s) | macro-economic |
| socio-economic(s) | socioeconomic |
| online, website, worldwide | on-line, web site, world-wide |
| web page, web manager | webpage |
| co-operation, co-ordinate (in OECD names always: Co-operation) | cooperation |
| e-commerce, m-government ("E-commerce" at the start of a sentence or title) | ecommerce |

**Compound adjectives**
- Hyphenate before the noun (attributive): an evidence-based approach, a well-known economist, longer-term effects.
- No hyphen after the noun or a verb (predicate): "The recommendations are evidence based", "in the longer term".
- No hyphen after an -ly adverb: "a highly skilled workforce".

**Proper nouns and title case**
- Capitalise only the proper-noun part: Anglo-American, French-speaking.
- In title case, capitalise both parts of a hyphenated compound: *Evidence-Based Decision Making*. Never capitalise after a hyphen inside a single word: *Co-operation*, not *Co-Operation*.
- Joint works: OECD-FAO.

**Dangling hyphens**
- Allowed when clear, keeping every hyphen: "pre- and post-pandemic data"; "first-, second- and third-year students".
- Avoid them when they confuse: write "undergraduate and postgraduate", not "under- and postgraduate".

**Multiple hyphens and age ranges**
- Multiple hyphens are fine when needed (balance-of-payment deficits), but rephrase heavy chains.
- Age range as an adjective: "15-to-24-year-old workers". As a noun: "15-19 year-olds".

**Prefixes**
- Follow established usage (Oxford dictionary): upfront, underdeveloped, overestimate, rewrite.
- Hyphenate when clarity needs it: cross-country, intra-industry, non-tradeable.
- Hyphenate to avoid ambiguity: re-sign vs resign; co-op vs coop.
- Hyphenate doubled vowels or letters: co-ordinate, re-entry, non-negotiable, shell-like.

**Compass points**
- No hyphen in Northeast, Southwest and the like.
- Use "Central and Eastern European", not "Eastern European", for OECD Members.
