# OECD writing style

Write and edit English text so it reads as OECD content: clear, plain, formal, British English, precise with numbers and names, consistent from start to finish. The rules come from the *OECD Style Guide* (fourth edition, 2025). They apply to every format: reports, papers, web, social media, internal documents.

## How to use this skill

1. **Work out the content type**, because some rules depend on it:
   - Reports and books use title case for their titles. Chapters, papers, briefs, case studies, boxes, figures and tables use sentence case.
   - Web content always writes numbers as digits.
   - Contractions are allowed only on social media where characters are limited.
   - If it isn't clear and it matters, ask, or assume and say so.
2. **Load the reference file for the topic in hand** (see the list below). The core rules on this page cover most drafting. Go to the references for tables, edge cases and citation formats.
3. **Draft, or edit**, applying the rules.
4. **Report back** (see "Telling the user what changed").

## Core rules (apply to every text)

**Voice and words**
- Write plain, clear English for a global, often non-native audience. Prefer the short common word. Avoid foreign and Latin expressions where English has an alternative: "among other things", not "inter alia".
- Don't refer to the OECD with "we", "our" or "us" in formal writing. Write "OECD analysis shows…", "the Organisation".
- Avoid vague or ambiguous words: "quite", "rather", "somewhat", "respectively", "the former/the latter", "address" (be specific), "across" (when ambiguous), "and/or", "impact" as a verb, "presently", "scheme", "slated", "table" as a verb, "regime" for a government, "including but not limited to".
- Use "annex", not "appendix". "Data" is plural.

**Spelling**
- British English, as in the Concise Oxford English Dictionary:
  - -ise endings: organise, analyse
  - -our endings: labour
  - -re endings: centre
  - doubled consonants: travelling, labelled, focussed
  - programme
  - Keep US spelling in names that use it: International Labour Organization, World Health Organization.
- Hyphenation: policymaker and policymaking, decision maker and decision making (no hyphen), socio-economic, co-operation, online, website, web page, worldwide.

**Names**
- Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) on first use, then "OECD" or "the Organisation". Capitalise "OECD Member(s)", "Member country/countries" and "non-Member". Capitalise "Key Partners" (Brazil, China, India, Indonesia, South Africa). Lowercase "accession candidate country" and "observer". Use "OECD area" for the region, not "OECD Members".
- Countries:
  - Use the official OECD names: Türkiye, Czechia, the Slovak Republic, Korea, China (People's Republic of) in lists.
  - "The" goes before the Netherlands, the Russian Federation, the Slovak Republic, the United Kingdom and the United States, and each takes a singular verb.
  - Spell the name out as a noun (the United States); abbreviate it as an adjective (US firms).
  - List countries alphabetically unless there's a reason for another order, such as a ranking.
  - Write "euro area" in lowercase.
- Acronyms: spell out at first use with the acronym in parentheses. Only introduce one if the term recurs. Write it in capitals with no full stops. Plurals take no apostrophe (IOs). Use "the" only for letter-by-letter acronyms used as nouns (the UN, UNESCO, OECD experts).

**Capitalisation and type**
- Sentence case for headings, chapter, paper, brief, box, figure and table titles. Title case only for report, book, periodical and series titles, project names, and country notes when naming the parent publication. After a colon in a title, capitalise the first word.
- Capitalise specific names (the Council Decision on…, the Ministerial Council Meeting); lowercase generic uses (a decision, the government, ministers).
- Italics for titles of reports, books, journals, newspapers, databases and paper series, and for untranslated foreign terms. Roman type in double quotation marks for chapter, paper, brief and article titles. Don't use bold or underlining for emphasis or hierarchy. Bold only, very sparingly, when meaning depends on it.

**Numbers**
- Words for one to ten, digits for 11 and above. Use digits even below ten with %, ISO currency codes, units, "million/billion/trillion", scales, and when mixed with numbers above ten. Use words at the start of a sentence and in titles. Web content uses digits throughout.
- Separate thousands with a (hard) space: `20 000`, `1 275 103`. Years and page numbers take no space.
- Decimal point, never a comma. Leading zero: `0.45`.
- Percentages:
  - `%` with no space (`5%`), except in titles and at the start of a sentence ("Ninety per cent…").
  - One decimal, consistently (`2.0%`, `2.2%`).
  - Repeat `%` in a series (`45%, 55% and 65%`). A hyphenated range takes one `%` (`10-20%`).
  - Write "percentage points (p.p.)" in full at first use.
- Currency: ISO code, hard space, amount (`USD 5 million`, `EUR 20`). Use the generic term for generic references ("millions of dollars"). Don't mix codes, symbols and names.
- Units: hard space before the unit (`3 km`, `80 tonnes`), standard symbols only, °C only (no Fahrenheit).
- Round large numbers in text (`1.2 billion`).

**Dates and periods**
- Dates: `15 April 2024`, with no ordinals and no commas. Times use the 24-hour clock (`14:30`). Spell months and days out in full, except in tight tables and figures.
- Years: decades as `1990s`; ranges as `2019-2024` (all four digits, hyphen); a period straddling two calendar years as `2025/26`.
- Quarters: "the fourth quarter of 2024" in text; `Q2 2020-Q1 2021` for ranges, tables and figures.
- Use months or quarters rather than seasons.

**Punctuation**
- No Oxford comma ("Japan, Australia and New Zealand"), unless it's needed for clarity.
- No em-dashes (—). Use spaced en-dashes ( – ) in pairs, or a single one. No dashes of any kind in titles.
- Use double quotation marks, with single marks only for a quote within a quote. Full stops and commas go inside the marks only if they belong to the quoted text.
- No contractions (cannot, do not) outside space-limited social media. No exclamation marks in formal text. No ampersand in running text (except R&D or official names). No slash for "or". No space before : ; ? !
- One colon per sentence, and lowercase after it except in titles. Semi-colons only for related clauses or lists whose items contain commas.
- Lists:
  - Items that are complete sentences start with a capital and end with a full stop.
  - Fragments start lowercase and take no punctuation.
  - Keep items grammatically parallel.

**Sources**
- In text, cite author-date: `(OECD, 2024)`, `(Deng and Mi, 2023)`, `(Hardy et al., 2024)` with four or more authors, and several works separated by semi-colons. In OECD production files the citation also carries a bracketed reference number, `(OECD, 2024[1])`.
- Never put a source in a footnote or endnote. Every source cited must appear in the reference list. Formats are in `references/citations-notes.md`.
- Every box, figure, infographic and table gets a source line below it: `Source: Author (Year), Title, URL.` Never write "Source: OECD" or "Source: Authors".

**Inclusive language**
- Gender-neutral terms: chair, workforce, police officer.
- Use singular "they" when gender is unknown, or rephrase.
- "Older people", not "the elderly" or "seniors".
- People-first disability language, with no "suffers from".
- "Minority" as an adjective (minority groups).
- Specific rather than generic ethnic or national terms.

## Things you cannot check alone

Some rules depend on internal OECD lists you don't have:
- the LEG list of official country and territory names
- the exact territorial disclaimers (for Israel, Kosovo and Cyprus)
- AGORA terminology
- the O.N.E Author styles

When text touches these, apply what you know. Flag the point for the user to verify, for example: "Check the name against the LEG list" or "Insert the official territorial disclaimer for Israel". Don't invent the wording.

## Hard spaces

The guide requires non-breaking spaces in these places:
- inside numbers (`20 000`)
- between a number and its unit, currency code or noun (`USD 10`, `37 firms`, `page 10`)
- between day and month (`15 April`)
- in country names (`United States`)
- between a title and a name (`Mr Smith`)
- around ellipses

In files the user will publish or paste into Word (`.docx`, `.md`, `.html`), write these as U+00A0. In a chat reply, ordinary spaces are fine unless the user asks otherwise. Mention once that hard spaces should be applied in O.N.E Author/Word (Ctrl+Shift+Space).

## Telling the user what changed

Users may knowingly prefer something else, such as American spelling, two decimals, an Oxford comma or "Turkey". That's their choice, but they must always be told when text departs from OECD style.

- **Drafting new text**: follow the rules. If the user asked for something that breaks a rule, do what they asked. End your reply with a short **"Departures from OECD style"** list: one line each, giving what was used, the rule, and the compliant form.
- **Editing the user's text**:
  - Correct it silently in the text, then add a concise **"OECD style changes"** summary grouped by type, e.g. "Spelling: -ize to -ise (6)", "Numbers: 2,500 to 2 500 (3)", "Removed Oxford commas (4)".
  - Show individual before/after pairs for substantive changes: names, terms, meaning-bearing words.
  - If the user wants a tracked-changes style or a list instead of a rewrite, give that.
- **Reviewing only** (no rewrite requested): list each issue with its location, the rule and the fix, most important first: names and legal sensitivities, then accuracy (numbers, citations), then consistency, then minor style.
- Leave quotations alone (except obvious typos, flagged), along with titles of external works and official names that use their own spelling. Say so if they look non-compliant.
- Once a user confirms a departure, don't keep flagging it in the same conversation.
- If the text was already compliant, say so in one line.

## French

This skill is for English. If the user drafts in French, note that OECD French style differs:
- spaces before `: ; ? !` and `%`
- « guillemets »
- ISO codes after the number (`150 EUR`)
- sentence case for the full forms of acronyms
- no plural -s on acronyms

`references/spelling-usage-inclusive.md` lists common English/French false friends that trip up bilingual drafters.
